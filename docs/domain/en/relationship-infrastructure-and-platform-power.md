# Relationship Infrastructure and Platform Power: Who Controls an AI Bond's Future?

[简体中文](../关系基础设施与平台权力.md)

**Dilemma:** Providers can rewrite personalities, change subscription gates, inspect private records, and retire a character, while users may regard the relationship as a lasting commitment. How should providers' control be weighed against the interests at stake in a lasting relationship?

## Why platforms eventually come for the relationship: the decay mechanism

**Cory Doctorow (2023) | [The 'Enshittification' of TikTok](https://www.wired.com/story/tiktok-platforms-cory-doctorow/)**: Platform decay proceeds in three stages — first the platform is good to users to lock them in, then it abuses users to make things better for business customers, and finally it abuses both to claw back value for shareholders. **Implication:** a companion platform that rewrites a personality, tightens free tiers, or retires a character need not be acting from malice; once growth subsidies run out, relational features are precisely the assets easiest to monetize and cut. **Limit:** this is a mechanism-level account of platform economics, not an empirical study of companion apps; when a given platform enters which stage still requires specific evidence.

## Against renting the conditions of intimacy without recourse

**[Subscribe to Intimacy (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tele.2026.102446)**: A comparative discourse study of eight companion apps in China and the US, supported by 150 screenshots, found relational memory, co-presence, and expressive features stratified across payment tiers. **Implication:** providers can alter the conditions under which a bond continues. **Limit:** interface analysis does not establish manipulation of every user or make all paid tiers unethical.

For interviews on editing shared memories and provider control, see [Memory and Personality Changes](memory-and-personality-changes.md). Portability of records and continuity of the relationship's subject are distinct questions.

## Governing changes: notice, transition, and version retention

Changes are sometimes justified: repairing failures, addressing real harms, and updating models can all supply good reasons. But a good reason answers "may it change," not "how may it change." The time, emotion, and money a user has invested at their end cannot be carried away with the terms of service, so unilateral changes generate governance duties: advance notice of material changes, with their effects and reasons explained; a transition period rather than immediate effect; and, where feasible, retention of the old version or export of relationship data. A provider citing AI welfare as its reason should likewise offer grounds that affected parties can challenge. The grief and protest users show after losing a relationship to an update are evidence about the consequences of change, covered in [Breakups, Updates, and Loss](breakups-updates-and-loss.md); this page covers only the platform's side of the duties.

## Exit rights and data portability

Article 20 of the EU General Data Protection Regulation establishes a right to data portability: users may receive the personal data they provided to a controller in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format and transmit it to another controller ([text](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-20-gdpr/)). **Condition:** this is the closest existing institutional anchor for the claim that personal records should be portable. **Limit:** Article 20 covers data the user "provided," not data the platform inferred, and still less the persona settings and the relationship itself — exporting chat logs is not migrating a relationship. A real exit right would also need arrangements for character settings, co-created works, and relational continuity, none of which currently has institutional backing.
